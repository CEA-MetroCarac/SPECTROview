    1. Executive Summary¶

    An empirical audit and profiling study of SPECTROview's Vectorized Batch Fit (VBF) Engine was conducted across real Raman (3_3721map.wdf) and cathodoluminescence (1_CL_map.txt) hyperspectral datasets. The investigation explains why runtime escalates sharply when switching from Lorentzian to Pseudo-Voigt profiles or adding multiple overlapping peaks, and separates per-iteration computational cost from convergence-count inflation.

    Key Findings¶

    1. Benchmark Group A and Group B Test Different Spectra and Solver Tolerances:

    2. Group A (1_CL_map*.json) crops [319.70..480.79] (M=102 on 3_3721map.wdf, M=304 on 1_CL_map.txt). On 3_3721map.wdf, [319.70..480.79] is a signal-free region (med_peak = 6.81, frac_neg = 62.2%), causing unguided fitting into baseline noise (R²_med <= 0.00). On the intended dataset 1_CL_map.txt, real CL peaks sit near 387.7–394.8 (R²_med = 0.9841–0.9906).

    3. Group B (3_3721map*.json) crops [460.23..570.28] (M=71) or [460.23..579.64] (M=77), centered on the strong silicon Raman peak at 521.73 cm⁻¹ (med_peak = 6,695.98, R²_med = 0.9988–0.9996).

    4. Furthermore, 1_CL_map.json and 3_3721map.json use coef_noise = 1 (which zeroes all weights on signal-free spectra, skipping 3,477/3,721 fits on 3_3721map.wdf), whereas the multi-peak and Pseudo-Voigt configs set coef_noise = 0.0 (fitting every spectrum, including noise).

    5. Convergence Cost Dominates Multi-Peak Slowdowns (25× Iteration Multiplier), Amplified by Bound-Clipping:

    6. A single well-conditioned Lorentzian (3_3721map.json) or Pseudo-Voigt (3_3721map_1pseudo.json) converges in 4 iterations.

    7. Adding two weak satellite peaks alongside the Si peak (3_3721map_3Lorent.json, starting with Peak 1 at x0=497.15, a=0.0, w=0.10) stalls convergence: only 44/3,721 (1.2%) converge within max_ite = 100, forcing 99.3% mean active rows across all 100 iterations (a 25× iteration multiplier over the 1-peak case: 0.315 s -> 7.419 s).

    8. Verified Root Cause (P1.1): In optimizer.py, the convergence check measures np.abs(dp) before box-bound projection (np.clip(p_active + dp, lo, hi)). When any peak amplitude or PseudoVoigt alpha hits 0.0 (verified directly: 1,494 rows pinned at P1_alpha=0.0 on 1_CL_map_1pseudovoigt.json), |p_trial - p| == 0 and cost improvement is 0, yet the unconstrained Newton step dp remains nonzero, blocking convergence until max_ite. Fixing this single line yields a 4.14× speedup on 3_3721map_3Lorent.json (8.620 s -> 2.082 s, convergence rising from 44/3,721 to 3,486/3,721) and 1.84× on 1_CL_map_1pseudovoigt.json.

    9. Per-Iteration Cost Drivers (Fixed-10-Iteration Controlled Measurements):

    10. Why 1 Pseudo-Voigt is 3.18×–4.23× slower per iteration than 1 Lorentzian: batched_pseudovoigt() and batched_pseudovoigt_jac() recompute batched_gaussian(), batched_lorentzian(), batched_gaussian_jac(), and batched_lorentzian_jac() independently — recalculating dx, dx², 1/w², and normalized offset t² four times per Jacobian call and allocating temporary (N, M) and (N, M, 3) buffers before copying into (N, M, 4). Fixed-10-iteration cost grows 46.54 ms -> 148.13 ms (3.18×) on 3,721×77 and 576.95 ms -> 2,438.98 ms (4.23×) on 16,384×304.

    11. Why VBFevaluator.jacobian() accounts for ~50–60% of self+cumulative time: In cProfile, jacobian() spends more self time copying slices (_to_full(), np.zeros(), J_full[:, :, slc] = jac_fn(...), and fancy-index extraction J_full[:, :, self._free_idx]) than models.py spends computing derivatives. At N=16,384, M=304, 6_Lor (K=18) over 10 fixed iterations, JacCopy = 2,685.81 ms actually exceeds JacMath = 2,114.60 ms.
    12. _batched_solve() Cholesky Crossover Cliff (Na <= 500, K >= 10): As tail spectra stay active and drop below 501 rows while K >= 10, _batched_solve() switches from C batched np.linalg.solve to a Python loop calling scipy.linalg.cho_factor / cho_solve, creating a measured 5.7×–33.8× per-call regression (Na=450, K=12: 89.883 ms vs 2.661 ms).
    13. Working-Set Growth & Cache Spilling (O(N * M * K)): Holding full active tensors in DRAM instead of L2/L3 cache-sized batches forces repeated DRAM round-trips for residuals, Jacobians, and normal equations; additionally, full-axis dense evaluation wastes compute far outside localized Raman peaks.
    14. Cold Linear Baseline Import: First-ever invocation of Linear baseline fitting triggers a cold from scipy.interpolate import interp1d costing 1,508.64 ms (0.75 ms warm).

    3. Priority 1 Action Plan (Immediate Target — High Impact, Low Complexity)¶

    These five pure-NumPy/Python changes preserve existing public contracts, exact numerical precision (float64), and startup import deferral:

    • P1.1 (optimizer.py) — Post-Clip Projected-Step Convergence + Uniform-Weight Fast Path:

    • Compute dp_actual = p_trial_active[improved_active] - p_active[improved_active] (after np.clip box-bound projection) inside dp_rel rather than raw unconstrained dp. Note that p_active = p[active_idx] via integer array indexing is already a separate copy before p[improved_idx] is updated.

    • Detect once up front when weights is None or (weights == 1.0).all() to bypass weights * residuals and J_r *= weights[:, :, None] array passes.

    • P1.2 (optimizer.py) — Unify _batched_solve on Vectorized np.linalg.solve:

    • Always try np.linalg.solve(A, b[..., None])[..., 0] across all (Na, K, K) systems regardless of Na or K, eliminating the 5.7×–33.8× per-matrix Cholesky loop cliff at Na <= 500, K >= 10. Retain per-matrix cho_factor/np.linalg.solve/0.0 strictly inside the except np.linalg.LinAlgError fallback for singular edge cases.

    • P1.3 (models.py) — Fuse batched_pseudovoigt & batched_pseudovoigt_jac + Optional out= Buffer:

    • Share dx, inv_w, inv_w2, dx2, t2 = dx2 * inv_w2, g_unit = exp(-4*ln2*t2), and invD = 1 / (4*t2 + 1) inside single-pass fused batched_pseudovoigt and batched_pseudovoigt_jac.

    • Add optional out=None slice support to batched peak/Jacobian evaluators so VBFevaluator writes directly into preallocated buffers without per-peak allocations. Measured speedup: 1.71×–1.85×, numerical deviation max_abs_diff = 3.47e-12.

    • P1.4 (evaluator.py) — Zero-Copy VBFevaluator.jacobian() & evaluate() Free-Param Fast Path:

    • Precompute self._all_free_identity when no parameters are fixed (_fixed_idx is empty) and no parameter expressions (_exprs) exist.

    • Skip _to_full(), skip np.zeros() zero-initialization (every column 0..K-1 is partitioned cleanly by peak slices), pass out=J[:, :, slc] directly to peak evaluators, and return J without J_full[:, :, _free_idx] fancy-index copying. Support an optional caller-supplied out= buffer in evaluate() and jacobian() when not self._exprs.

    • P1.5 (evaluator.py, baseline.py) — O(N) Noise-Threshold Lookup & Pure-NumPy Linear Baseline:

    • Replace dists = np.abs(x[None, :] - x0_vals[:, None]) in VBFevaluator.apply_noise_threshold() with uniform fast-path (when x0 is identical across N) or np.searchsorted + neighbor compare on monotonic 1D x, cutting (N, M) memory down to O(N).

    4. Phase 2 Implementation Plan (High Impact, Moderate Complexity)¶

    Phase 2 targets memory hierarchy efficiency, stalled-peak termination, localized spatial sparsity, and multi-core CPU scaling — all purely in Python/NumPy without native compiler toolchains.

    P2.1 — L3-Cache-Sized Batch Chunking & Active-Row Compaction [Partially Completed & Shipped in v26.48.1 — commit 9250ae9]¶

    Implementation Status (9250ae9, v26.48.1): Adaptive L3-cache batch chunking (_choose_chunk_size(N, M, K) in optimizer.py:21–42, chunked outer loop in optimizer.py:263–320 with monotonic weighted progress_callback), physical (K, N, M) Jacobian buffer transposed to (N, M, K) for unit-stride BLAS dgemm, in-place analytical derivatives in models.py, zero-copy all_active = (n_active == N) full-batch fast path, and 2D noise/moving-average memoization in noise.py are completed and shipped. Under controlled idle-CPU re-benchmarking (v26.48.2), WDF 3_3721map.json (3,721 × 70, K=3) improved from 157.6 ms (Phase 1) to 70.81 ms (63.67 ms with OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=2), and CL 1_CL_map.json (16,384 × 302, K=3) improved from 3,802.1 ms (Phase 1) to 3,433.74 ms (1,434.02 ms with OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=2). Only intra-chunk active-row sub-array compaction when 1 <= n_diverged < N_chunk remains open.

    Problem & Evidence¶

    • Currently, batched_levenberg_marquardt() keeps global (N, M, K) Jacobian tensors (~114–684 MiB on N=16,384, M=304) and (N, K, K) caches for all N spectra simultaneously, even after 75–90% have converged or while active rows span fragmented memory indices.
    • Every iteration on non-contiguous active_idx pays cache misses across the full N-row allocation.
    • Empirically verified on 3_3721map.wdf / 3_3721map_3Lorent.json: splitting N=3,721 into chunk=512 reduced runtime from 8.254 s -> 4.942 s (1.67×) with max |ΔR²| = 2.22e-16.

    Design & Touchpoints (optimizer.py, vbf_engine.py)¶

    1. Deterministic Adaptive Chunk Sizing (_choose_chunk_size(N, M, K)):

    2. Target a working set (chunk_size, M, K) * 8 bytes <= ~8–16 MiB (fitting comfortably inside L3 cache on typical 8–32 MiB CPU caches while staying big enough for BLAS matmul batch amortization).

    3. Clamp chunk_size in [256, 1024] (e.g., chunk_size = max(256, min(1024, (12 * 1024 * 1024) // max(M * max(K, 1) * 8, 1)))). If N <= chunk_size, run zero-split direct path to avoid overhead on small calls.

    4. Chunk Execution & Contiguous Active-Row Compaction:

    5. Slice (x_chunk, y_chunk, p0_chunk, w_chunk) into contiguous chunks [s:e], call _batched_lm_core(...), and write back into preallocated (N, K) p_out, (N,) converged, and (N,) n_iter arrays.

    6. Inside each chunk Nc <= 1024, compute noise/zero-weight masks locally. Once active count drops cleanly (or when active density < 0.5), optionally pack (p_c, residuals_c, JTJ_c, JTr_c, lam_c) into dense contiguous sub-arrays, avoiding scattered active_idx / dirty[active_idx] gather/scatter penalties.

    7. Note: Each spectrum in LM is mathematically 100% independent, so chunking preserves bit-exact parity (max |ΔR²| ~ 1e-16).

    8. Progress Reporting Integration (progress_callback):

    9. Maintain weighted completed-spectrum counts (completed_in_finished_chunks + converged_in_current_chunk) so UI progress bars inside VBFthread remain smooth and monotonic (0 -> 100%) instead of resetting per chunk.

    Risks & Validation¶

    • Shared x vs 2D (N, M) x: Always slice x[s:e] when x.ndim == 2, pass x unmodified when x.ndim == 1.
    • Test: Assert chunked (chunk=256, 512) vs unchunked output parity (np.allclose on p, R², converged, n_iter) across 1D and 2D x, weights, and _exprs.

    P2.2 — Inactive / Bound-Pinned Peak Convergence & Stagnation Early Exit¶

    Problem & Evidence¶

    • Even after P1.1 fixes bound clipping for pixels pinned right at iteration t, multi-peak configurations with an absent/vanishing component (like Peak 1 x0=497.15, a=0.0 in 3_3721map_3Lorent or 2nd/3rd peak on background CL pixels) still suffer from ill-conditioned (J^T J) blocks: when ampli ≈ 0, derivatives \(\partial/\partial x_0 = 0\) and \(\partial/\partial w = 0\), leaving zero columns in J (rank < K) so LM spends dozens of iterations shrinking/growing damping \(\lambda\) before xtol/ftol triggers.
    • On 1_CL_map.txt / 1_CL_map_3lorentzian, ~22.6% of spectra still hit max_ite=100 (61.4 s even after P1.1 + P1.4).

    Design & Touchpoints (optimizer.py, fit_engine/)¶

    1. Diagonal Tikhonov Floor / Zero-Column Safeguarding:

    2. In (JTJ + lam * diag(JTJ)), a dead peak with ampli == 0 yields diag(JTJ)[idx] == 0. Current lam * np.maximum(diag_JTJ, 1e-6) helps invertibility, but when a peak amplitude collapses to 0 (ampli <= lo_ampli + eps and gradient w.r.t. ampli points negative/outward), zeroing the trial steps of its coupled (x0, fwhm, alpha) parameters prevents spurious width/position drift and triggers immediate step acceptance or convergence (dp_actual == 0).

    3. Stagnation / Consecutive Rejected-Step Early Exit:

    4. Currently, a spectrum stops early only when lam >= 1e7 (optimizer.py:310) or when rel_cost_change < ftol & dp_rel < effective_xtol.

    5. Under floating-point precision plateaus near 1e-7..1e-12, tiny 1e-8 cost improvements can keep a row spinning at lam = 1e2..1e6 for 80+ iterations with zero physically meaningful movement.

    6. Add two safe early exits (configurable / preserving strict precision tests):

      • Plateau Exit: Track consecutive tiny-cost-and-tiny-step accepted iterations (e.g., rel_cost_change < ftol * 0.1 for 3 consecutive accepted steps -> converge).
      • Dead-Step Rejection Cap: Keep (worsened_active & lam_trial >= 1e7) while additionally terminating rows whose unconstrained np.linalg.norm(dp) would move < 1e-12 or which spend > 12 consecutive rejections without improvement.

    7. Optional Exact Backtracking / Nielsen LM Damping Update:

    8. Replace heuristic factor 10.0 / 3.0 (optimizer.py) with Nielsen's gain-ratio (rho) update (mu *= max(1/3, 1 - (2*rho - 1)**3), nu = 2 vs mu *= nu, nu *= 2), which reduces rejected step ratios on coupled multi-peak landscapes by avoiding premature damping spikes and overshoot restarts.

    Risks & Validation¶

    • Tight-tolerance unit tests: Any test expecting max_ite or exact tight xtol=1e-10 on synthetic noise-free problems must still pass — gate Nielsen / stagnation exits cleanly so xtol/ftol contracts are respected when tight tolerances are explicitly requested.

    P2.3 — Safe Support-Windowed Peak Evaluation (|x - x0| <= c * fwhm)¶

    Problem & Evidence¶

    • Raman and sharp luminescence spectra often span wide wave-number ranges (M = 500–2,000) where individual peaks have fwhm = 4–12 cm⁻¹. Away from |x - x0| > 6 * fwhm, Gaussian weights decay below 2^-144 (exact zero in float64), whereas Lorentzian tails decay slowly (\(1 / (1 + 4 (dx/w)^2)\): 1/65 ≈ 1.54% at 4*w and 1/10,001 ≈ 10⁻⁴ only at 50*w).
    • Benchmark from vbf_bench_part2.txt (6 Lorentzians, N=1681, M=1024, fwhm=6): dense float64 = 966.43 ms/iter vs +-4*w block-diagonal windowed 82.72 ms/iter (11.68×) when localized!

    Critical Mathematical Guardrail (Why Support Windowing Requires Care)¶

    • Lorentzian / Asymmetric Lorentzian / PseudoVoigt have heavy algebraic tails (\(O((w/dx)^2)\)):

    • Cutting Lorentzian strictly at 4 * fwhm leaves a 1.54% truncation discontinuity if used directly on evaluate() / residuals. Even at 15 * fwhm, 1 / (1 + 4*225) = 0.0011 (0.11%).

    • Therefore, a naive hard cutoff on models.py for Lorentzian/PseudoVoigt without tail correction would distort broadband fits (R² drift on wide peaks).

    • Safe Design Strategy:

    • Exponential Models (Gaussian, GaussianAsym): Exact machine-epsilon cutoff exists at |dx / w| > 4.5 (exp(-4 * ln(2) * 4.5^2) = 3.1e-25 < eps_float64). A slice/index window around [min(x0) - 5*max(w), max(x0) + 5*max(w)] for each chunk/peak group is mathematically exact to machine precision (< 1e-16).

    • All-Peak ROI Tight-Mask Prefilter (vbf_engine.py / VBFevaluator): Within any chunk, compute [lo_chunk, hi_chunk] across active centers ± tail tolerance; when peaks all occupy a narrow active sub-band inside an uncropped spectrum (or when DecaySingleExp/DecayBiExp only operate post-t0), restrict evaluation slice [i0:i1] for Jacobian & residual assembly.
    • Opt-In Fast Preview vs Exact Final Polish: Optionally use narrower fast support (8 * fwhm) during coarse early iterations if exposed as a precision mode, followed by full-band exact LM polish (1–2 iterations) to eliminate tail bias completely (ΔR² == 0.0).

    5. Phase 3 Implementation Plan (Advanced Architectural & Algorithmic Improvements)¶

    Phase 3 narrows the remaining architectural gap between Python/NumPy tensor batching and compiled L1-resident single-spectrum C++ loops (such as HORIBA LabSpec 6's OlMainer.dll).

    P3.1 — Optional Compiled Native C++/OpenMP L1-Resident Kernel (Cross-Platform)¶

    Architectural Rationale¶

    Why can a compiled C++ loop (OpenMP parallel for per spectrum) beat whole-map Python tensor ops even when NumPy calls BLAS/C?

    • Cache Residency: For one spectrum of size M = 100..500, K = 3..18, the working set (x, y, r, J, JTJ, JTr) is ~10–80 KiB — fitting 100% inside per-core L1/L2 CPU cache, never hitting DRAM inside the LM loop.
    • Loop Fusion: Evaluating f(x), r, J, AND accumulating symmetric upper triangle of JᵀJ (0.5 * K * (K+1) entries) and Jᵀr inside a single streaming pass over i = 0..M-1 eliminates (N, M, K) memory materialization completely (from ~684 MiB peak DRAM down to < 1 MiB!) and fuses transcendental exp() SIMD instructions with normal-equation GEMM.
    • Zero Per-Iteration Barrier: Each CPU thread iterates its own subset of spectra cleanly to convergence with zero inter-iteration synchronization and zero active-mask compaction.

    Implementation Blueprint¶

    1. Backend Design: Write a clean, portable C++17 + OpenMP (using PyBind11 or Cython) module _vbf_native covering:

    2. Built-in peak kernels (Gaussian, Lorentzian, PseudoVoigt, GaussianAsym, LorentzianAsym, Fano, DecaySingleExp, DecayBiExp) — no arbitrary Python string callbacks inside the hot C++ loop.

    3. Direct Cholesky (LL^T) / LDL^T symmetric positive-definite linear solver on stack-allocated <= 64×64 buffers (matching VBF's setStackSize lesson — fixed small stack buffers or per-thread heap scratch reused across all spectra).

    4. Identical parameterization, bounds clipping, linear/soft_l1/huber robust weights, and ftol/xtol/max_ite convergence semantics matching optimizer.py.

    5. Hybrid Dispatch & Automatic Fallback:

    6. In VBFengine.fit_spectra(): check not evaluator._exprs and all peak models supported by native kernel + _vbf_native importable (try: import spectroview.fit_engine._vbf_native).

    7. Otherwise seamlessly fall back to Phase 1/2 NumPy/Python optimizer.py (which continues supporting arbitrary user _exprs, dynamic/custom scalar models, and pure-Python environments without a C compiler).

    8. Packaging & Cross-Platform Distribution:

    9. Distribute prebuilt Python wheels (cibuildwheel in CI) for Windows x64, macOS arm64/x64 (libomp), and Linux x64 so end-users never require build tools locally; keep SPECTROview 100% runnable in pure Python if the compiled extension isn't built in dev mode.

    P3.2 — Variable Projection (VarPro / Separable Nonlinear Least Squares) & Optional Spatial Warm-Start¶

    Mathematical & Algorithmic Concept¶

    Every spectroscopic multi-peak sum is linear in peak amplitudes \(a_1, \dots, a_P\) given nonlinear shape parameters \(\theta = \{(x_{0,p}, w_p, \alpha_p)\}_{p=1}^P\):

    \[\mathbf{y} \approx \sum_{p=1}^{P} a_p \phi_p(\mathbf{x}; \theta_p) = \Phi(\theta) \mathbf{a}\]

    1. Variable Projection (VarPro):

    2. Instead of optimizing \(K = 3P\) (or \(4P\)) parameters jointly (where strongly correlated overlapping peak amplitudes oscillate with widths/centers and produce poorly conditioned JᵀJ), reduce LM unknowns to nonlinear shape parameters only (\(2P\) or \(3P\), e.g. 6 instead of 9 for 3 Lorentzians, 9 instead of 12 for 3 Pseudo-Voigts — reducing \(K^3\) solve cost by ~3× and conditioning dramatically).

    3. For each spectrum trial \(\theta\), solve the small non-negative / unconstrained \((P \times P)\) system \(\Phi^T \Phi \mathbf{a} = \Phi^T \mathbf{y}\) (or Kaufman's approximated VarPro Jacobian for \(\theta\)).

    4. Requires careful handling of fixed/expressed amplitudes and upper/lower amplitude bounds (nnls or bounded active-set when lo/hi constrain amplitudes); therefore suited as a well-tested specialized solver path for standard unbounded/non-negative sum-of-peaks models.

    5. Optional Spatial Warm-Start Propagation (Grid-Aware Coarse Sweep -> Batched Refine):

    6. On smoothly varying Raman/wafer maps (x_coord, y_coord), spectra close in space often share very similar peak centers/widths while p0 builds a single global scalar guess across all N rows.

    7. Instead of sequential spiral fitting (which destroys parallelism), use a 2-stage parallel refinement:

      1. Fit a coarse downsampled grid (e.g. every \(4 \times 4\) tile or \(k\)-means spectral cluster centroids, ~5% of spectra) cleanly to convergence.
      2. Broadcast/bilinearly interpolate converged parameters to initialize the remaining 95% of pixels, which then run in 1 fully vectorized batched LM pass (typically converging in 2–4 iterations instead of 30–100).
      3. Must stay strictly opt-in or map-only (MapData with uniform spatial grid / spectral clustering fallback) with careful handling of domain boundaries / discontinuities (e.g., defects or empty substrate regions via residual-gated acceptance: keep spatial seed only if cost(spatial_p0) < cost(default_p0)!).

    P3.3 — Vectorized Forward-Mode Dual-Number AD for Linked Expressions (_exprs) [Completed & Shipped in v26.48.1 — commit 9250ae9]¶

    Implementation Status (9250ae9, v26.48.1): Fully completed and shipped in spectroview/fit_engine/evaluator.py via _DualVar, _DUAL_SAFE_NAMESPACE, topological expression bytecode compilation (_compiled_expr_ops), and batched np.matmul chain-rule combination (2.97× speedup over finite-difference chain-rule loops), verified by unit tests in tests/unit/fit_engine/test_evaluator.py.
